A [[general election]] is scheduled to be held on 28 November 2026 to elect members of the 61st [[Parliament of Victoria]]. All 88 seats in the [[Victorian Legislative Assembly|Legislative Assembly]] and all 40 seats in the [[Victorian Legislative Council|Legislative Council]] will be up for election.

The incumbent [[Victorian Labor Party|Labor]] [[Carroll ministry|government]], led by [[Premier of Victoria|Premier]] [[Ben Carroll]], will seek to win a fourth consecutive four-year term against the [[Opposition (Victoria)|opposition]], the [[Liberal–National Coalition (Victoria)|Liberal–National Coalition]], led by [[Leader of the Opposition (Victoria)|Opposition Leader]] [[Jess Wilson]]. Other parties including [[One Nation]], the [[Victorian Greens|Greens]] and [[Independent (politician)|independents]] will also participate in the election. 

The election will be administered by the [[Victorian Electoral Commission]] (VEC).

== Background ==
=== Previous election and parliament ===
{{See also|Results of the 2022 Victorian state election (Legislative Assembly)

{{See also|Results of the 2022 Victorian state election (Legislative Council)|Members of the Victorian Legislative Council, 2022–2026}}

The [[Victorian Labor Party|Labor Party]], led by [[Daniel Andrews]], was elected into government at the [[2014 Victorian state election|2014 election]]. Labor has been in government for all but one term since the [[1999 Victorian state election|1999 election]]. Andrews' government was re-elected at the [[2018 Victorian state election|2018 election]] and again at the [[2022 Victorian state election|2022 election]], winning 56 seats. The [[Liberal–National Coalition (Victoria)|Liberal–National Coalition]] gained one seat to 28 seats, the [[Australian Greens Victoria|Greens]] won 4 seats. In the [[Legislative Council of Victoria|Legislative Council]], the Labor party won 15 of the 40 seats.{{Citation needed|date=October 2026}}

Andrews announced his resignation as premier and leader of the Labor Party on 26 September 2023, the resignation being formally effected the following day. This precipitated a [[2023 Victorian Labor Party leadership election|leadership election]] within the Labor party-room, which was won unopposed by Deputy Premier [[Jacinta Allan]] following hours of intense negotiations between members of Labor's [[Labor Left|left]] and [[Labor Right|right]] factions. Allan, of the left, was elected leader, with right-faction member and Education Minister [[Ben Carroll]] as her deputy.<ref>{{cite news |title=Jacinta Allan fends off late challenge to become 49th premier of Victoria |url=https://www.abc.net.au/news/2023-09-27/jacinta-allan-to-succeed-daniel-andrews-as-victorian-premier/102906268 |access-date=27 September 2023 |work=ABC News |date=27 September 2023 |language=en-AU |archive-date=27 September 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230927050753/https://www.abc.net.au/news/2023-09-27/jacinta-allan-to-succeed-daniel-andrews-as-victorian-premier/102906268 |url-status=live }}</ref> Allan became the second female premier in the state's history, following [[Joan Kirner]]'s 1990–1992 premiership.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.theguardian.com/australia-news/2023/sep/27/new-premier-victoria-jacinta-allan-daniel-andrews-resignation-labor |title=Jacina Allan to become premier of Victoria |last1=Kolovos |first1=Benita |last2=Ore |first2=Adeshola |work=[[The Guardian]] |date=27 September 2023 |accessdate=27 September 2023 |archive-date=27 September 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230927044156/https://www.theguardian.com/australia-news/2023/sep/27/new-premier-victoria-jacinta-allan-daniel-andrews-resignation-labor |url-status=live }}</ref> The government is seeking a fourth consecutive four-year term.{{Citation needed|date=October 2026}}

Following the Liberal/National Coalition's defeat, Opposition Leader and Liberal leader [[Matthew Guy]] announced in his post-election [[concession speech]] he would resign the leadership of the party. This ensured a [[2022 Victorian Liberal Party leadership election|leadership election]] was held for the position, at which newly elected member for [[Electoral district of Hawthorn|Hawthorn]] [[John Pesutto]] defeated [[Brad Battin]] by one vote in a secret ballot of Liberal party-room MPs.<ref>{{cite news|first=Gus |last=McCubbing |title=John Pesutto wins Liberal leadership in Victoria by one vote |url=https://www.afr.com/politics/john-pesutto-wins-liberal-leadership-in-victoria-by-one-vote-20221208-p5c4ot |access-date=8 December 2022 |work=Australian Financial Review |date=8 December 2022}}</ref>

Over the course of the parliament's term, there was one [[2023 Narracan state supplementary election|January 2023 supplementary election in Narracan]] following the death of a candidate ahead of the state election, and five by-elections, namely the [[2023 Warrandyte state by-election|August 2023 Warrandyte by-election]], the [[2023 Mulgrave state by-election|November 2023 Mulgrave by-election]], the February 2025 [[2025 Werribee state by-election|Werribee]] and [[2025 Prahran state by-election|Prahran]] by-elections, and the [[2026 Nepean state by-election|May 2026 Nepean by-election]]. In only the seat of Prahran was there a change in party representation, with the Greens losing the seat to the Liberals, whilst in the four other districts the incumbent party retained the seat.

Aside from these by-elections, the Assembly's composition was altered by the removal of [[Will Fowles]] and [[Darren Cheeseman]] from the Labor caucus in October 2023 and April 2024 respectively, with both moving to the [[crossbench]] as "[[Independent Labor (Australia)|Independent Labor]]" MPs. The resignation of [[Sam Hibbins]] from the [[Australian Greens Victoria|Greens]] on 1 November 2024 also resulted in him briefly sitting on the crossbench as an [[Independent politician|independent]] MP, until his resignation from the parliament later that month, causing the aforementioned by-election in Prahran.<ref>{{cite web |author=Andy Yu |date=23 November 2024 |title=Prahran MP Sam Hibbins announces he is quitting parliament |url=https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024-11-23/prahran-mp-sam-hibbins-resigns-from-parliament/104639568 |work=ABC News}}</ref>

On 27 December 2024, a [[2024 Victorian Liberal Party leadership spill|leadership spill]] resulted in [[Brad Battin]] replacing Pesutto as Liberal leader and leader of the opposition.<ref>{{Cite news |date=26 December 2024 |title=Victorian Liberals elect Brad Battin as new leader |url=https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024-12-27/vic-libs-john-pesutto-moira-deeming-brad-battin-jess-wilson/104764726 |access-date=27 December 2024 |work=ABC News |language=en-AU}}</ref> Pesutto's leadership came under intense pressure after a federal court judgement finding he defamed Liberal parliamentarian [[Moira Deeming]] and was ordered to pay $300,000 in damages.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/australia-news/2024/dec/12/moira-deeming-wins-defamation-case-victorian-liberal-leader-john-pesutto-over-neo-nazi-comments-ntwnfb|title=John Pesutto defies calls to resign after being ordered to pay $300,000 for defaming Moira Deeming|author=Benita Kolovos|date=12 December 2024|work=The Guardian}}</ref> Battin's leadership lasted only eleven months, as he was defeated in another [[2025 Victorian Liberal Party leadership spill|leadership spill]], held on 18 November 2025, by [[Electoral district of Kew|Kew]] MP [[Jess Wilson]].<ref>{{Cite news |date=17 November 2025 |title=Jess Wilson becomes first woman to lead Victorian Liberals |url=https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-11-18/victorian-liberal-leadership-spill-battin-wilson/106021260 |access-date=17 November 2025 |work=ABC News |language=en-AU}}</ref> Media reporting quoted several Liberal MPs [[Source (journalism)|on background]] complaining about Battin's alleged lack of focus on issues other than Victoria's [[Crime in Victoria|crime rates]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://au.news.yahoo.com/liberals-seek-fresh-face-rise-164556462.html|title='New direction' as Liberals crown first woman leader|work=AAP|publisher=Yahoo News|date=18 November 2025|author=Callum Godde}}</ref>

=== Rise of One Nation ===

By early 2026, the right-wing [[One Nation]] party's polling had risen considerably, with many [[Opinion polling for the next Australian federal election|national polls showing them on a higher vote than the Liberal Party]]. One Nation polled more votes than the [[South Australian Liberal Party|Liberal Party]] at the [[2026 South Australian state election]], winning four seats to the Liberal Party's five. [[SA Labor]] meanwhile won in a landslide.{{Citation needed|date=October 2026}}

Liberal Party members have had differing views of how to counter the One Nation surge, with former Premier [[Jeff Kennett]] urging the party to form an 'anti-Labor partnership' with One Nation. Meanwhile, Victorian Liberal president Phil Davis has warned his party against chasing One Nation "to the fringe of Australian politics", publicly chastising [[Peta Credlin]] and other Liberal members from its [[National Right (Liberal Party of Australia)|conservative faction]] for running an agenda "through Sky News and News Corp newspapers to divide and remake the party."<ref> {{Cite web |last=Grand |first=Chip Le |date=6 April 2026 |title=Liberals 'doomed if they try to become One Nation-lite' |url=https://www.theage.com.au/politics/victoria/liberals-doomed-at-victorian-election-if-they-try-to-become-one-nation-lite-warns-party-boss-20260406-p5zlm4.html |access-date=4 June 2026 |website=The Age |language=en}}</ref>

== Electoral system ==

Eligible Victorian electors are required to cast a ballot due to [[compulsory voting]] laws. The eligibility criteria for enrolment to vote includes being 18 years or older, an Australian citizen, and to have lived in Victoria for longer than a month.<ref>{{cite web |title=State elections |url=https://www.vec.vic.gov.au/voting/types-of-elections/state-elections |website=[[Victorian Electoral Commission]] |access-date=4 November 2022 |archive-date=12 August 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230812014801/https://vec.vic.gov.au/voting/types-of-elections/state-elections |url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Legislative Assembly ===

For the election of members to [[single seats]] of the [[Victorian Legislative Assembly|Legislative Assembly]], the [[Victorian Electoral Commission]] (VEC) uses [[Instant-runoff voting|full preferential voting]] where for a vote to count, it is required to number every box on the ballot in the order of the most preferred candidate for the particular [[Electoral districts of Victoria|electoral district]] an elector is registered to vote in.<ref name="VEC">{{cite web |title=Preferential voting |url=https://www.vec.vic.gov.au/voting/how-voting-works/preferential-voting |website=[[Victorian Electoral Commission]] |access-date=6 November 2022 |language=en |archive-date=7 November 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221107000646/https://www.vec.vic.gov.au/voting/how-voting-works/preferential-voting |url-status=live}}</ref> The election will cover all 88 Seats of the Victorian Legislative Assembly.<ref>{{cite web |title=State districts |url=https://www.vec.vic.gov.au/electoral-boundaries/state-districts |website=[[Victorian Electoral Commission]] |access-date=6 November 2022 |language=en |archive-date=7 November 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221107000644/https://www.vec.vic.gov.au/electoral-boundaries/state-districts |url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Legislative Council ===
In the [[Victorian Legislative Council|Legislative Council]], five members are elected from each of the state's eight electoral [[Electoral regions of Victoria|regions]], ensuring a total of 40 members are elected to serve in the council. The eight regions each cover eleven lower house seats, resulting in uneven population distributions between the regions.<ref name=ConversationUpperHouse>{{cite web|url=https://theconversation.com/wholl-hold-power-in-the-victorian-upper-house-after-the-state-election-294349|title=Who'll hold power in the Victorian upper house after the state election?|date=5 November 2026|work=The Conversation|author=Zareh Ghazarian}}</ref> At this election, a voter is required to vote either above the line for at least five parties or grouping of candidates in order of their preference; or vote below the line for at least five individual candidates in order of their preference. This creates a form of [[proportional representation]], using the [[single transferable vote]] and [[optional preferential voting]] mechanisms.<ref name=ConversationUpperHouse/>

====Reforms to council's electoral system====
Ahead of this election the parliament passed [[electoral reform]] legislation to change the electoral system for the Legislative Council.<ref name="Kolovos-2026">{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/australia-news/2026/aug/12/victorian-microparty-mps-including-georgie-purcell-to-face-re-election-struggle-after-group-voting-tickets-abolished|title=Victorian microparty MPs including Georgie Purcell to face re-election struggle after group voting tickets abolished|work=The Guardian|date=12 August 2026|author=Benita Kolovos}}</ref> Under the former system that was first adopted for the [[2006 Victorian state election|2006 state election]], members were elected in one of the eight regions by electors who could vote either above the line or below the line,<ref name="VEC" /><ref>{{cite web |title=State regions |url=https://www.vec.vic.gov.au/electoral-boundaries/state-regions |website=[[Victorian Electoral Commission]] |access-date=6 November 2022 |language=en |archive-date=7 November 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221107000650/https://www.vec.vic.gov.au/electoral-boundaries/state-regions |url-status=live}}</ref> however above-the-line voters could only vote for only one party or grouping of candidates. Preferences were then automatically distributed based on the registered preference order provided to the VEC by the party-submitted [[group voting ticket]].<ref name="VEC" /><ref name="Kolovos-2026"/> When voting below the line voters were required to number a minimum of five boxes on the ballot in order of preference.<ref name="VEC" /> 

By 2026, Victoria's upper house had become the last remaining state or territory legislative chamber to utilise these group voting tickets to automatically distribute voter's above-the-line preferences,<ref name="Kolovos-2024">{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/australia-news/2024/oct/29/victoria-upper-house-election-changes-preferences-labor-inquiry|title=Elections for Victoria's upper house could be overhauled under proposals to stamp out 'preference whispering'|work=[[Guardian Australia|The Guardian]]|date=29 October 2024|author=Benita Kolovos}}</ref> with [[Western Australia]] abolishing the practice ahead of the [[2025 Western Australian state election#Legislative Council voting changes|2025 state election]]. Victoria's system had led to election results in the upper house becoming unrepresentative of the actual votes received, with so-called 'preference whisperers' such as [[Glenn Druery]] organising ideologically disparate [[Microparty|microparties]] to direct their preferences to each other, allowing candidates to be elected despite receiving less than 1% of the vote in some cases.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://antonygreen.com.au/how-gvts-have-distorted-victorian-legislative-council-elections/ |title=How GVTs Have Distorted Victorian Legislative Council Elections |website=Antony Green's Election Blog |last1=Green |first1=Antony |author-link=Antony Green |date=11 August 2026 |accessdate=11 August 2026}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theage.com.au/politics/victoria/the-dark-art-of-election-preference-harvesting-comes-under-scrutiny-20230222-p5cmpk.html |title=The dark art of election preference harvesting comes under scrutiny |website=The Age |last1=Eddie |first1=Rachel |date=22 February 2023 |accessdate=11 August 2026}}</ref>

Attempts to reform the system to more closely reflect the system used for the [[Australian Senate]] and other state-based upper houses were rejected by former Premier [[Daniel Andrews]], who described proponents of change as being "quite heavily motivated by wanting to get more of their people elected".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.abc.net.au/news/2023-08-10/victoria-microparties-upper-house-voting-election-system/102708204|title=Integrity of Victoria's upper house voting system a major concern for voters, inquiry submissions show|author=Richard Willingham|date=10 August 2023|work=ABC News}}</ref> Submissions to the post-2022 state election parliamentary inquiry calling for the abolition of the automatic above-the-line preference distributions eventually led to the parliament's Electoral Matters Committee (EMC) to examine the issue.<ref name="Nicholas-2025"/> Throughout 2025 the EMC oversaw an inquiry into reforming the electoral voting method for the Legislative Council.<ref name="Nicholas-2025">{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/news/ng-interactive/2025/dec/01/voting-in-victoria-is-broken-heres-how-it-could-be-fixed-and-who-would-benefit|title=Voting in Victoria is broken. Here's how it could be fixed and who would benefit|work=The Guardian|date=2 December 2025|author=Josh Nicholas, Benita Kolovos, Andy Ball, Nick Evershed and Ben Raue}}</ref> Any reform required the approval of both houses of parliament, whilst an alteration to the structure of the upper house, such as the number of members elected to the chamber or the eight regions from which they are elected, would require a vote at a state-wide [[referendum]].<ref name="Kolovos-2024"/><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.parliament.vic.gov.au/electoralsysteminquiry|title=Inquiry into Victoria's Upper House electoral system|work=parliament.vic.gov.au}}</ref> 

The EMC handed down its report in December 2025, recommending that group voting tickets be abolished and the "weighted inclusive Gregory method for surplus vote transfers" be adopted prior to the 2026 election.<ref name="parliament.vic.gov.au-2025">{{cite web|url=https://www.parliament.vic.gov.au/4ad9a5/contentassets/3d48e4bf35d841f0972a6f8d0b893cec/emc-60-03_vic-upper-house-electoral-system.pdf|title=Inquiry into Victoria's Upper House electoral system|work=Electoral Matters Committee|publisher=parliament.vic.gov.au|date=2 December 2025}}</ref> The committee further recommended a new process be established, involving either an expert panel, a citizens assembly or a constitutional convention, to consider changes to the electoral structure of the council which could be considered by voters at a referendum in 2028 or 2030.<ref name="parliament.vic.gov.au-2025"/>

The issue received renewed scrutiny in April 2026, when [[far-right]] commentator and former IDF soldier [[Avi Yemini]] announced his intention to set up the "[[Palestinian statehood|Free Palestine]]" party in a bid to attract votes from [[left-wing]] voters and redirect preferences of votes the party receives to [[right-wing]] parties.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-04-30/avi-yemini-free-palestine-political-party-concerns-experts/106618082|title='Free Palestine' party pitched by YouTuber Avi Yemini to funnel votes to conservatives|work=ABC News|author=Leanne Wong|date=30 April 2026}}</ref> Other [[Fake party|fake parties]] such as Climate Action Now, registered by anti-lockdown and COVID-19 conspiracy theorist [[Monica Smit]], and I'm Voting to Avoid the Fine, founded by her brother, were also registered ahead of the election with the intention to preference votes to parties such as [[One Nation]] and [[Family First Party (2021)|Family First]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=New political parties are exploiting Victoria's archaic electoral system |url=https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-07-15/victoria-election-group-voting-tickets-one-nation/106914588 |last=Briggs |first=Casey |date=2026-07-15 |website=ABC News}}</ref> 

The electoral commission responded by confirming it had no authority to prevent parties from adopting misleading names, and in June the commission began the process of registering "Free Palestine" as a political party.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-06-06/victorian-state-politics-analysis-upper-house-voting/106765794|title=Why Victoria's upper house voting regime leaves much to be desired, allowing secret backroom deals and preference swaps|author=Richard Willingham|date=6 June 2026|work=ABC News}}</ref> In July 2026 ''[[The Age]]'' reported that the [[Jacinta Allan ministry|Allan government]] was set to introduce legislation by the end of the month to abolish group voting tickets.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.smh.com.au/politics/victoria/election-law-overhaul-set-to-hurt-crossbenchers-and-help-one-nation-20260711-p60ehj.html|title=Election law overhaul set to hurt crossbenchers – and help One Nation|author=Kieran Rooney and Patrick Hatch|work=The Age|date=11 July 2026|url-access=subscription}}</ref>

The government introduced the ''Electoral Amendment (Miscellaneous Matters) Bill 2026'' to the parliament on 28 July.<ref name="legislation.vic.gov.au">{{cite web|url=https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/bills/electoral-amendment-miscellaneous-matters-bill-2026|title=Electoral Amendment (Miscellaneous Matters) Bill 2026|work=legislation.vic.gov.au}}</ref> The bill required voters to number at least five boxes for parties above the line, in order of preference, or else number at least five boxes for individual candidates below the line.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/bills/601335exi1.pdf|title=Explanatory Memorandum: Electoral Amendment (Miscellaneous Matters) Bill 2026|work=legislation.vic.gov.au|quote=Clause 15 substitutes section 93A(1), (2) and (3) of the Electoral Act 2002...New subsection (2) outlines the new proposed method of voting  above the line. It requires electors to sequentially number at least numbers 1 to 5 to indicate their preference by group, if there are more than 5 groups or if there are 5 groups or less, by numbering all the squares consecutively from number 1 in order of preference. New subsection (3) outlines the method of voting below the line, which is to vote by candidates. It requires electors to consecutively number at least numbers 1 to 5 to indicate their preference by candidates and is the same as the existing method for voting for candidates.|page=4}}</ref> Like the system in place for the Senate, a savings provision is incorporated so as to enable voters who mark a clear 1 but fail to number their top five preferences clearly, to still have their vote counted as formal.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.tallyroom.com.au/65241|title=The end of group voting tickets – bill revealed|date=29 July 2026|author=Ben Raue|work=The Tally Room}}</ref> The electoral commission is also tasked with publishing information about newly registered political parties, a response to Yemini and other's efforts to establish parties titled with generally misleading names.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/making-victorias-elections-fairer-and-more-transparent|title=Making Victoria's Elections Fairer and More Transparent|date=29 July 2026|work=premier.vic.gov.au}}</ref> The bill passed the [[Legislative Assembly of Victoria|Legislative Assembly]] without any recorded objections  on 29 July, and passed the [[Legislative Council of Victoria|Legislative Council]] by 33 votes to 4 on 11 August 2026.<ref name="Kolovos-2026"/><ref name="legislation.vic.gov.au"/><ref>{{cite web|url=https://kevinbonham.blogspot.com/2026/07/group-ticket-voting-going-going-rolling.html|title=Group Ticket Voting: Going, Going ... ? (Rolling Thread)|date=28 July 2026|work=Dr Kevin Bonham}}</ref> The legislation received [[royal assent]] on 18 August 2026 and went into effect the following day.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-08/26-028aa-authorised.pdf|title=Electoral Amendment (Miscellaneous Matters) Act 2026|work=legislation.vic.gov.au}}</ref>

==== Chris Moore ====
On 11 November, candidate for [[Electoral district of Greenvale|Greenvale]], Chris Moore, apologised over a series of offensive Islamophobic, [[racist]], [[Sexism|sexist]], and [[Homophobia|homophobic]] remarks he made on social media between 2010 and 2012. These comments included that he "doesn’t like the way the new homo cleaner at work looks at me", calling [[Prime Minister of Australia|former-prime minister]] [[Julia Gillard]] a "slut", repeated references to [[Indian Australians]] as "curry munchers," as well as sharing a photo of a woman and a child both wearing a [[burqa]] next to two full plastic garbage bags with the caption "Haha, spot the difference."

The electorate, which is a fairly safe Labor seat, has a large [[Middle East|Middle Eastern]] and [[West Asia|West Asian]] population and one of the highest proportions of people born in the Middle East. In response, a Labor party spokesperson said that the comments were "reprehensible" and that there was "no place for racist, Islamophobic and sexist comments," with the Liberal party saying that it would put the candidate last on its how-to-vote card for the electorate. One Nation did not disendorse Moore, who apologised for the remarks saying that the comments were unthoughtful and that they "don't reflect who I am today."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Belot |first=Henry |last2=Kolovos |first2=Benita |date=2026-11-11 |title=One Nation Victoria candidate apologises for slew of offensive comments about lesbians, Indians and Julia Gillard |url=https://www.theguardian.com/australia-news/2026/nov/11/one-nation-candidate-apologises-offensive-comments-ntwnfb?CMP=Share_iOSApp_Other}}</ref>
